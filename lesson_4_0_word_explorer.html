Focus on Patterns

Learn through radicals and character families

1. 女字旁：先看偏旁，再猜意思

When you see 女字旁, pause and ask: “Could this character be connected to women, marriage, family roles, or old social expectations?” This clue is not a perfect rule, but it is a very helpful first guess.

嫂sǎoolder brother’s wife / sister-in-law
嫂子sǎoziolder brother’s wife; sister-in-law
妇fùwoman / married woman
妇女fùnǚwomen
媳xídaughter-in-law
媳妇xífùwife / daughter-in-law
婆póolder woman / mother-in-law
公婆gōngpóhusband’s parents
Student tip: Do not memorize each word as a lonely word. Put them into a small visual family: 嫂 / 妇 / 媳 / 婆. 女字旁 helps you quickly notice that many of these words are connected to women, family identity, marriage, or historical family relationships.
Guess from the radical: The character 嫂 most likely refers to which meaning?

2. 米字旁：看到“米”，先想到米、食物和质地

When you see 米字旁, first think of rice, grain, food, powder, or texture. For example, 糯 gives you a strong clue: it is connected to sticky / glutinous rice. Then look at the whole word, because the full word may have a more specific meaning.

糯nuòsticky / glutinous
糯米nuòmǐsticky rice
糕gāocake / pastry
糟糕zāogāoterrible
米mǐrice
粉fěnpowder
Student tip: Use 米字旁 as a first clue, not the final answer. If you see 糯, you can guess: “This probably has something to do with rice or texture.” Then 糯米 confirms the meaning: sticky / glutinous rice.
Guess from the radical: The character 糯 most likely has something to do with what?

3. 木字旁：先猜“树木植物”，再看词义变化

When you see 木字旁, your first guess can be “tree, wood, or plant.” 柳 is a clear example because it means willow. Some characters with 木 later developed more abstract meanings, so after your first guess, always check the whole word and context.

柳liǔwillow
柳树liǔshùwillow tree
树 / 樹shùtree
木mùwood / tree
权 / 權quánpower / right
权利 / 權利quánlìrights
Student tip: Start with the easiest clue first. 柳 has 木字旁, so you can guess it may be a tree or plant — and it is: willow. For harder words like 权 / 權, 木 is still part of the character, but the modern meaning is abstract: power / rights.
Guess from the radical: The character 柳 most likely means what?

Similar Words · 近义词区分

在意 vs 在乎

Both can be translated as “care,” but they feel different in Chinese. Pay attention to register, emotional weight, and the relationship between speaker and object.

1. Core Difference 核心区别

👉 在意 = mind / care, often with a negative or cautious feeling. It sounds more reasonable, restrained, and focused on judgment or details.
👉 在乎 = care deeply. It sounds more emotional, personal, and relationship-centered.
词 Register / Style 感觉 常见场景
在意
zàiyì		 more rational / restrained
偏理性、克制		 有点介意；注意到；关心细节 小事、评价、别人怎么看、细节、形象、成绩
在乎
zàihu		 more emotional / personal
偏情感、更个人		 很重视；很关心；情感投入更强 人、关系、感情、家人、朋友、爱情、重要价值
Memory tip:
在意 = 头脑 thinking / noticing / minding
在乎 = 心 feeling / emotional importance

2. About Other People’s Opinions 别人的看法

✓ 我不在意别人怎么说。
I don’t mind what others say. This sounds natural, calm, and reasonable.

△ 我不在乎别人怎么说。
This is possible, but stronger. It may sound like “I don’t care about anyone’s opinion at all.” The tone is more intense.

Use 不在意 when you want to sound calm and mature.
Use 不在乎 when the emotion is stronger or you want to sound more determined.

3. About People and Relationships 人和关系

✓ 我很在乎你。
I really care about you. This is emotional and relationship-based.

△ 我很在意你。
This does not usually mean “I love/care about you deeply.” It sounds more like “I notice/pay attention to everything about you,” or “your details affect me.” Depending on context, it can feel sensitive or slightly awkward.

在意 → pays attention / cares about details
在乎 → emotional importance

4. Negative Sentences 否定句很关键

✓ 我不在意这件事。
Neutral and calm. “I don’t really mind this.”

△ 我不在乎这件事。
Stronger. It can sound almost cold, indifferent, or like “It doesn’t matter to me at all.”

Subtle difference:
不在乎 often has a feeling of “无所谓 / 不关心.”
If you want to sound softer, say: 我不太在意。

5. Common Mistake 常见错误

✗ 我不在乎。
Students often say this directly, but it may sound cold, careless, or emotionally distant.

✓ 我不太在意。
Softer and more socially comfortable: “I don’t really mind.”

✓ 我不是很在意别人怎么看我。
A natural way to say you are not overly affected by others’ opinions.

Classroom reminder:
Before using 不在乎, ask: Do I want to sound strong, emotional, or indifferent? If not, 不太在意 is usually safer.

6. Mini Practice 小练习

Choose the better word:

1. 我不太 ______ 别人的看法。
Answer: 在意

2. 我真的很 ______ 我的家人。
Answer: 在乎

3. 她很 ______ 自己的发音和声调。
Answer: 在意

4. 你是不是还很 ______ 他？
Answer: 在乎

5. A softer way to say “I don’t care much” is:
我不太在意。

Usage Guide · 用法辨析

Words students commonly confuse or misuse

These words appear in Lesson 4.0 and are frequently misused by English-speaking learners — either because of a misleading English translation or because they look or sound similar to another word.

1
见不得 VS 见不得人
Two different phrases — not the same!
见不得 (jiàn bu dé) = "cannot stand to see; cannot tolerate the sight of." Used when you dislike or are disturbed by something.

见不得人 (jiànbudé rén) = "shameful; not fit to be seen by others; embarrassing." Describes something morally improper or disgraceful — it has nothing to do with literally "seeing people."
✓ 她见不得别人受苦。
She cannot stand to see others suffer.
✓ 宋朝以前，再嫁并不是什么见不得人的事。
Before the Song Dynasty, remarrying was not a shameful thing.
✗ 她做了见不得的事。
❌ Incomplete — must say 见不得人 (shameful deed).
2
至此
A formal written transition — not everyday speech
至此 (zhì cǐ) = "at this point; up to here; by now; thus far" — a formal written transition marker signaling that a described process has reached a stage or conclusion. It is almost exclusively used in written Chinese and would sound unusual in speech. Common alternatives in speech: 到这里, 就这样, 这样一来.
✎ Written 至此，中国基本上实现了男女平等。
At this point, China had essentially achieved gender equality.
💡 至此 is often followed by a summary statement. It marks a milestone in a narrative or argument, similar to "thus" or "by this point" in English academic writing.
✗ 至此，我们去吃饭吧。
❌ Too casual — 至此 does not fit informal spoken contexts.
3
高于 / 低于 / 多于
The 于-comparison structure in formal writing
Adjective + 于 + noun = a formal comparison structure used in written Chinese, especially in reports and academic texts. It replaces the conversational 比 structure.

高于 (higher than)　· 　低于 (lower than)
多于 (more than)　 · 　少于 (fewer than)
大于 (greater than) · 　小于 (less than)

Adding 远 (far/much) intensifies: 远高于, 远低于, 远多于.
✎ Written 男性的平均收入仍然远高于女性。
Men's average income remains far higher than women's.
💡 Spoken equivalent: 男性的平均收入比女性高得多。
(Same meaning, more conversational.)
